Counting, position and Turkish signs

I give vision models a photo and ask short questions with one clear answer: "How many swans are there?", "Is the red bag on the left or the right?", "What does the sign say?". The answer key was written before any model was tried, and scoring is done by code on the raw answer, not by reading the screen.

CategoryWhat it checksExample
Countingobjects, and subsets of them (only the white ones, only the standing ones)How many bicycles are standing?
Colour / positioncolours, left/right, front/behind, scene details, things that are not thereWhich side is the bench on?
Turkish textsigns, boards and screens with ç, ğ, ı, İ, ö, ş, üATATÜRK BULVARI vs ATATURK BULVARİ
  • Questions: 193 in total. Each question carries a source label: 113 are labelled kaynak=insan and 80 kaynak=ai. The two groups are scored and reported separately.
  • Photos: 27 scenes. 13 are my own photos (Samsun, Ankara, Istanbul, my classroom), which are not on the internet, so a model cannot have memorised them; the other 14 come from other sources.
  • Same conditions: every model gets the same photo and the same message: cloud models through the Gemini API (temperature 0, free tier), local models through Ollama on my laptop. Provider, settings and time are logged with every answer.
  • Turkish-aware scoring: "ISTANBUL" is not "İSTANBUL", but suffixes are fine: "mavi" also matches "mavidir", "sol" matches "solunda". I added the suffix rule after reading the raw outputs and re-scored every model with it.

* Free-tier quota and server load: these models answered only part of the set, scored on the questions they answered.

  • Big gap between cloud and small models: the Gemini family scored 78–95%, the best local model (qwen2.5vl, 3 GB) 40%.
  • Counting is the weak spot of the lighter Gemini models (63–69%), while the same models get 85–88% on colour and position.
  • Reading Turkish signs is where small models break: qwen2.5vl and gemma-4-26b 16%, granite 0%.
  • Small local models struggle with Turkish questions: moondream answered in English ("Yes") and skipped questions; llava gave vague answers like "a few people" instead of a number.
  • Gemma models often "thought" until they hit the token limit and left questions unanswered, which counts as wrong.
  • Questions labelled insan were harder: 41.7% average across models vs 48.3% on ai questions.
  • Cost: every run used free tiers (0 $), so the chart compares speed instead of price.

Questions stay private, scores are public. The question set is a gated dataset on Hugging Face (access on request) so it does not leak into training data. A 50-question open demo set is in nexa-bench.
